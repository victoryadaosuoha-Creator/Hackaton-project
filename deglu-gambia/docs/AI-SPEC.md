Déglu Gambia — AI Responsibilities Project AI specification · Hackathon version The AI in Déglu Gambia should act as an intelligent civic assistant that helps citizens report community problems, helps institutions understand and manage those reports, and makes communication between citizens and institutions easier. 1. What the AI should do 1. Understand citizens' reports The AI reads a report written by a citizen or transcribes a voice report, then identifies the actual problem being described. Example: “The streetlight near our school has not worked for three weeks.” The AI understands that the report concerns a broken streetlight. 2. Automatically categorise reports The AI suggests the most appropriate category, such as: Roads and infrastructure Water and sanitation Electricity and streetlights Waste management Healthcare Education Public safety Other community concerns Citizens or authorised staff should be able to correct a wrong category. 3. Detect potentially duplicate reports The AI compares new reports with existing reports to identify cases that may describe the same problem in the same area. Example: Ten citizens report the same overflowing rubbish bin. The AI flags the reports as potential duplicates so authorised staff can review and link them. It must not automatically delete reports or assume that similar reports are definitely the same incident. 4. Suggest urgency and priority The AI evaluates the report's wording, type of problem and available information to suggest whether it may be low, medium, high or urgent priority. Example: A dangerous exposed electrical wire near a school may require urgent attention. The AI provides a recommendation, not a guaranteed assessment. Authorised staff retain responsibility for confirming priority. 5. Support voice reporting Citizens who find typing difficult can speak their reports. Speech recognition converts the recording into text, and the AI helps organise that text into a clear report. The citizen must be able to review and correct the transcription before submitting it. Any additional language support must be tested rather than assumed. 6. Act as a civic information assistant The AI answers questions about how to use Déglu Gambia, how to submit a report, how to understand report statuses, and what information a citizen needs to provide. It should use verified information where available and admit when it does not know an answer. It must not pretend to be a government official or invent government policies. 7. Help institutions understand community problems The AI can summarise report trends, highlight frequently reported issues, and identify areas with many unresolved reports. Example: “The submitted reports show a recurring waste collection problem in this area.” These summaries should be based on actual report data, not invented statistics. 8. Help make reports clearer The AI can summarise long reports, extract useful details, and ask citizens for missing information before submission. It must preserve the citizen's original meaning and never add facts that the citizen did not provide. 2. How the AI fits into the app Citizen submits a report Typed text, voice transcription, location and optional photo AI analyses the report Categorises, summarises, checks for possible duplicates and suggests priority Report is submitted and routed The backend saves the report and routes it according to configured rules and authorised departments Institution reviews and acts Staff verify recommendations, update the report status and record progress Citizen receives updates The app displays verified status changes and available notifications 3. What the AI must NOT do It must not fabricate report statuses, statistics, government decisions or completed repairs. It must not automatically reject a citizen's report just because it resembles another report. It must not expose private citizen information or internal staff notes. It must not independently decide which government institution is legally responsible without configured routing rules or verification. It must not claim a problem has been fixed until an authorised update confirms it. It must not expose AI API keys or secret credentials in frontend code. 4. What we should implement first for the hackathon To keep the project realistic, I recommend prioritising these four AI capabilities: Automatic report categorisation Report summarisation and missing-detail suggestions Potential duplicate detection and priority suggestions A civic assistant and voice-reporting support One important distinction: these are the AI's responsibilities, not a claim that every feature is already implemented. Each feature must be connected to a real AI service and tested; if a feature is only simulated for the hackathon demo, it should be labelled accordingly
